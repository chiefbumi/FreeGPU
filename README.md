# Free-GPU
### Building a GPU for gamers, by gamers. Open source. Built to perform. Built to last.

## The Mission

Build a fully open-source gaming GPU that delivers extraordinary performance, lasts for decades, is easy to repair, and makes high-performance gaming affordable for everyone.

We believe great gaming shouldn't be limited to people who can afford expensive hardware.

Our goal is to make the technology available for anyone to study, improve, build, and manufacture.

This project is not about making the GPU with the highest synthetic benchmark score.

**It's about making a GPU that gamers actually love using.**

## The Big Goal

Build a GPU capable of playing modern AAA games at native 1080p resolution and a consistent 1,000 FPS, while being reliable and repairable enough to remain in service for 50 years.

### Target Specifications

| Category | Objective |
|---|---|
| Resolution | 1920 × 1080 (1080p) |
| Performance | 1,000 FPS minimum |
| Frame consistency | Frame times at or below 1 ms |
| Games | Fortnite, Call of Duty, NBA 2K, GTA VI |
| Service life | 50 years |
| Operation | Designed for 24/7 use |
| Reliability | Minimal failures and performance degradation |
| Repairability | Easy to disassemble, repair, and replace components |
| Affordability | Make high-performance gaming as inexpensive as possible |
| Open source | Public hardware designs, software, tools, and documentation |

These are research objectives, not specifications we have already achieved.

## Why Are We Building This?

Growing up playing video games, many of us dreamed of having the best gaming setup in the world.

We wanted higher FPS, lower latency, and smoother gameplay. But the best hardware was often too expensive.

We want to change that.

We believe open-source technology can make it easier for more people and companies to develop and manufacture hardware.

More competition can create opportunities for better products and lower prices.

We also believe gaming hardware should last.

A great GPU shouldn't become useless because a fan, memory component, or power module fails. Wherever technically possible, parts should be repairable and replaceable.

Think of the reliability of a Toyota, applied to computer hardware.

## Our Engineering Philosophy

**Start with the objective. Figure out how to achieve it through experimentation.**

We don't need to know every step before starting.

We believe in:

- Learning by building.
- Reading research papers, textbooks, and existing source code.
- Using the best available tools and creating new ones when necessary.
- Experimenting, making mistakes, and learning from them.
- Questioning existing designs and trying different approaches.
- Building for actual users, not just benchmark scores.
- Publishing our research, including experiments that don't work.

We're not committed to any single GPU architecture, programming language, manufacturing process, or design philosophy.

The objective determines what we need to learn and build.

The long-term goal remains the same. The methods evolve as we learn.

## How We'll Test Our GPU

**By playing actual games.**

Our primary test is the experience of using our hardware.

Games we want to test include:

- **Fortnite:** Competitive gameplay, building, editing, aiming, and responsiveness.
- **Call of Duty:** Story campaigns, detailed environments, intense combat, and multiplayer.
- **NBA 2K:** Smooth animations, responsive controls, and complete basketball games.
- **Grand Theft Auto VI:** Open-world exploration, driving, detailed environments, and gameplay, when a compatible version is available.

We want to play for hours and answer simple questions.

Does it feel smooth? Does it respond immediately? Does it stutter? Does it crash? Can we rely on it?

We'll measure FPS and frame times during actual gameplay to understand the experience.

We won't treat synthetic benchmark scores as the primary measure of success.

## Built to Last

Our long-term goal is a GPU system capable of staying operational for 50 years under everyday or continuous use, with maintenance and replacement of serviceable components when needed.

We want hardware that is:

- Extremely reliable.
- Easy to clean and maintain.
- Easy to take apart and reassemble.
- Designed around replaceable components wherever practical.
- Supported by publicly available repair instructions.
- Supported by open software for decades.

We will investigate how to make repairs faster, simpler, and less expensive without sacrificing performance.

The 50-year objective will require long-term reliability research and testing. It cannot be verified in ten weeks.

## Fully Open Source

Our intention is to publish everything we develop and have the rights to distribute:

- GPU architecture and hardware designs
- Source code and firmware
- Graphics drivers and compilers
- Circuit schematics and PCB designs
- Development tools
- Testing and experimental results
- Manufacturing and repair documentation
- Research notes, including failed experiments

Third-party tools and components will be credited and used according to their licenses.

We want engineers, students, researchers, gamers, and manufacturers to be able to contribute.

Commercial manufacturing and sales of compatible hardware are welcome under the project's eventual open-source licenses.

## Current Status

No final GPU architecture has been selected.

Performance and reliability claims will be published only when supported by actual results.
